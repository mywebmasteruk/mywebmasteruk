## The three metrics

| Metric | Measures | Good | Needs work | Poor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LCP | Loading — when the main content appears | ≤ 2.5s | 2.5–4.0s | > 4.0s |
| INP | Responsiveness — delay before the page reacts | ≤ 200ms | 200–500ms | > 500ms |
| CLS | Visual stability — how much the layout jumps | ≤ 0.1 | 0.1–0.25 | > 0.25 |

## Field data, not lab scores

The thresholds apply to the 75th percentile of real visits, gathered from actual users over a
rolling 28-day window. A perfect score in a local audit tool tells you the page *can* be fast;
it does not tell you it *is* fast for the quarter of your audience on a poor connection and an
older phone.

This is why a site can hold a near-perfect Lighthouse score and still fail Core Web Vitals in
Search Console. The two are measuring different things, and only one of them is what search
engines use.

## The cheapest wins

Most failures come from a small set of causes: images without width and height attributes
causing layout shift, render-blocking scripts delaying paint, fonts loading late and reflowing
text, and third-party tags executing on the main thread. Fixing those four covers the majority
of pages, and none of them require a redesign.
