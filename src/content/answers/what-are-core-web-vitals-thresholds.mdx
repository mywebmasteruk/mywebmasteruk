## The three metrics

| Metric | Measures | Good | Needs work | Poor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LCP | Loading — when the main content appears | ≤ 2.5s | 2.5–4.0s | > 4.0s |
| INP | Responsiveness — delay before the page reacts | ≤ 200ms | 200–500ms | > 500ms |
| CLS | Visual stability — how much the layout jumps | ≤ 0.1 | 0.1–0.25 | > 0.25 |

## Real visitors, not a testing tool

These are measured from your actual visitors, on their own phones and connections, over the
past month — and you have to be fast for roughly three quarters of them, not on average.

That is why a website can score almost perfectly in a speed-testing tool and still be marked
as failing by Google. The tool tells you the page *can* be fast on a good connection. Google is
telling you what it is actually like for the quarter of your customers on an older phone with
patchy signal. Only one of those is what search engines use.

## The cheapest wins

Most failures come down to four things: pictures that do not reserve their space, so
everything jumps down when they load; code that has to finish before anything appears; fonts
arriving late and reshuffling the text; and third-party extras — chat widgets, tracking, social
buttons — holding everything up. Fixing those four covers most websites, and none of them
require a redesign.
