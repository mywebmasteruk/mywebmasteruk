## The three bands

| Band | Typical UK price | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic upkeep | £30–£80 / month | Updates, backups, uptime monitoring, security patching |
| Managed support | £150–£500 / month | The above plus an allowance of developer hours for changes |
| Performance-managed | £500+ / month | The above plus measured work on search, speed and conversion |
| Ad-hoc | £50–£95 / hour | Fix-on-request, no retainer, no continuity |

<p class="mono muted">Ranges reflect commonly advertised UK rates and vary by platform and complexity.</p>

## Why hourly is usually the expensive option

Paying by the hour sounds cheaper because the number is smaller. In practice it quietly
punishes you for getting in touch: every message costs money, so small problems get left until
they are big ones. It also means nobody is looking at your site between the phone calls — and
the things that slowly damage your position in Google are quiet, invisible and expensive by the
time anyone notices.

## What actually justifies a higher price

Not more hours. The only good reason to pay more is that somebody is actually changing what
your website does for your business, and can show you exactly what they changed and what
happened next. Ask anyone you are considering one question: *what will be different about my
website three months from now?* If the answer is a list of tasks and a graph of visitor numbers,
you are buying activity, not results.
