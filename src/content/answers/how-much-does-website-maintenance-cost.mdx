## The three bands

| Band | Typical UK price | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic upkeep | £30–£80 / month | Updates, backups, uptime monitoring, security patching |
| Managed support | £150–£500 / month | The above plus an allowance of developer hours for changes |
| Performance-managed | £500+ / month | The above plus measured work on search, speed and conversion |
| Ad-hoc | £50–£95 / hour | Fix-on-request, no retainer, no continuity |

<p class="mono muted">Ranges reflect commonly advertised UK rates and vary by platform and complexity.</p>

## Why hourly is usually the expensive option

Hourly billing sounds cheaper because the number is smaller. In practice it creates a
disincentive to report problems: every message costs money, so small issues are left until
they are large ones. It also means nobody is watching the site between the calls — and the
things that erode search performance are slow, quiet and invisible until they are expensive.

## What actually justifies a higher price

Not more hours. The only defensible reason to pay more is that someone is measuring whether
the money produced a result, and can show you the number next to a group of pages they left
alone. Ask any prospective supplier one question: *how will we know at the end of the quarter
whether this worked?* If the answer is a traffic graph with no control, you are buying activity.
