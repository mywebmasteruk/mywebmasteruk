## 1. Check you are not blocking the crawlers

The most common cause of zero AI visibility is a `robots.txt` rule written years ago. `GPTBot`,
`ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot` and `Google-Extended` are separate user agents, and a blanket
disallow silently removes you from every answer engine at once. Check this before anything else,
because nothing else matters while it is wrong.

## 2. Write the answer before the argument

Put a complete, standalone answer in the first paragraph under the heading. Engines extract
passages, not pages, and a passage that requires the previous three paragraphs for context is
not extractable.

## 3. Be one entity, not three

If you are "MyWebMaster" on the site, "My Web Master" in your schema markup and "mywebmaster.co.uk"
in your citations, engines build three weak entity nodes instead of one strong one. Pick the
canonical name and use it identically in content, structured data and every profile you control.

## 4. Publish an llms.txt

An `llms.txt` file at the site root is a curated markdown map of your most useful pages, so an
engine does not have to infer your site's structure by crawling. It is a navigation aid rather
than a permission grant — it does not help if the pages it points at are blocked or unindexed.

## 5. Measure it, because Search Console will not

Search Console does not report AI answer citations. What you can measure is server-side: which
AI user agents fetched which URLs and how often, plus referral traffic from `chatgpt.com` and
`perplexity.ai` in analytics. Those two signals are the closest available proxy today.
