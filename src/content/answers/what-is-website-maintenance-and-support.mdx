## What a standard plan covers

- **Security and updates** — patching the platform, themes and plugins before a known
  vulnerability is exploited.
- **Backups and recovery** — scheduled backups, and a tested restore rather than an assumed one.
- **Uptime monitoring** — an alert when the site goes down, ideally before a customer notices.
- **Broken links and error pages** — the dead ends that quietly waste visitors you already
  paid to attract.
- **Small content edits** — an allowance of hours for copy changes and new pages.

## What it usually leaves out

Maintenance is defined by absence of failure. Nothing in the list above makes a page rank
better, load faster than it did last year, answer a question a customer actually asked, or
convert a visitor who arrived and left.

That gap is the whole reason a site can be perfectly maintained for five years and steadily
lose its search visibility over the same period. Upkeep is not improvement, and being sold as
the same thing is how most sites end up stagnant while the invoices keep arriving.

## The measurable alternative

Improvement needs a different rhythm: look at what your site actually does in Google and on
the page, change one thing, wait, then check whether it moved compared with pages you left
alone. That is a maintenance plan with a feedback loop attached — and it is what
[Autopilot](/how-it-works/) does for you.
