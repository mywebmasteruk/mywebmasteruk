## How answer engines choose what to cite

AI search engines use retrieval-augmented generation. A query triggers a conventional search
against an index, passages are extracted from the returned pages, and a language model writes
an answer from those passages with citations attached.

Two consequences follow. Your page must be indexable and retrievable, which is ordinary SEO.
And the specific passage must be clear enough to lift out of context, which is GEO.

## What makes a passage citable

- **It is self-contained.** A paragraph that fully answers one question without needing the
  rest of the page is the unit an engine can quote.
- **It is specific.** "Improves performance" is unquotable. "Reduced median load time from
  4.1s to 1.3s" is a sentence an engine can attribute to you.
- **It is attributed.** Claims that link to a primary source are cross-checkable, and content
  that survives cross-checking is cited more readily than content that does not.
- **It is structured.** Comparison tables, definitions and question-shaped headings are
  extracted more reliably than the same information buried in prose.

## The mistake to avoid

Fabricating statistics to look authoritative is worse than having none. Answer engines
cross-reference claims against what they already hold, and content that conflicts with known
data gets scored as untrustworthy — costing you the citations you would otherwise have earned.
