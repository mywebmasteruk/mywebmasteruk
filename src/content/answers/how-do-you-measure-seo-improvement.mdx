## Why "before and after" proves nothing

Visitor numbers move for all sorts of reasons that have nothing to do with anybody's work.
Google changes how it ranks things. A competitor's website goes down for a week. Your busy
season arrives. The way results are displayed changes.

So a graph that goes up after someone did some work is equally consistent with three very
different stories: the work helped, the work did nothing, or the work actively hurt and was
hidden by a rising tide. You genuinely cannot tell.

Most of this industry runs on that ambiguity, which is why so much SEO reporting can never be
proved wrong.

## Leaving pages alone on purpose

Pick pages that are genuinely comparable — similar type, similar purpose, similar traffic. Split
them at random into two groups. Only improve one group. At the end, report the difference.

This costs something real: the untouched pages do not get the improvements, so you are knowingly
leaving some value on the table. That is the price of a number you can actually trust, and it is
worth paying, because otherwise you can never tell a service that works from one that is simply
expensive.

## Decide the timescale before you look

Google's figures arrive about three days late, and a change takes weeks to settle into the
results. Anything measured over less than that is measuring random movement.

Deciding the timescale in advance — and not touching the page while the clock runs — is what
stops the result being chosen afterwards to make somebody look good.
