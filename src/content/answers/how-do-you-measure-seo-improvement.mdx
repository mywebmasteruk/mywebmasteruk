## Write the starting numbers down first

Before anything changes, record where the site stands: how often it appears in Google, how often
people click, how fast its pages really load, and how many enquiries it brings in. Put a date on
it.

Without that, the start date can be chosen afterwards — and a start date chosen once you already
know the result can make almost any piece of work look good.

## Predict each change before it goes live

Every change should come with one sentence written beforehand: what it is expected to do, and
why. *Shorter page titles should get more clicks, because the ending is currently cut off.*

A prediction written after the result is known is not a prediction. It is a caption.

## Give it weeks, not days

Google's figures arrive about three days late, and a change takes weeks to settle into the
results. Anything judged over a few days is judging random movement.

Decide the timescale in advance, and do not keep changing the page while the clock runs —
otherwise there is nothing left to read.

## Judge it by what the business needs

Total visitor numbers are the easiest thing to show and the least useful. What matters is whether
more of the right people are finding you for the searches that bring in work, and whether more of
them get in touch.

If a website genuinely needed changing, the difference should show up there — in enquiries — not
in a decimal point on a report.
