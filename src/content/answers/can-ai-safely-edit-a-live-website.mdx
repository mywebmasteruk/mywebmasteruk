## Safety is about the setup, not the cleverness

The instinct is to ask whether the AI is clever enough to be trusted. That is the wrong
question, because the answer changes every few months and you cannot check it yourself.

A better question: **what is the worst thing this could do to my website, and how fast can it be
undone?** Get the answer to that right and cleverness becomes a matter of how well it performs,
not whether it is dangerous.

## The three levels

**Things it just does.** Mechanical, instantly reversible, with no wording involved: fixing
links that lead nowhere, keeping your page list accurate, stopping pictures making the page jump
about, repairing labels that have stopped working. There is no version of these jobs that you
would want done differently.

**Things it asks about first.** Anything that changes what your business says: your headline in
Google, the wording on a page, a new page, a change to a form. These come to you with the
reasoning attached, and nothing happens until you say yes.

**Things it never touches.** Your prices, your guarantees, your legal wording, claims about your
business, and the settings that decide whether Google is allowed to show your site at all. One
wrong move on that last one can remove your entire website from Google, and you would not notice
for weeks. That is far too much damage to hand over, at any level of confidence.

## Being able to undo it beats being careful

A cautious system you cannot undo is more dangerous than a bolder one you can. Every change
should stand on its own, so putting it back takes seconds and disturbs nothing else — and there
should be a single instruction that stops everything, which does not depend on one particular
person being at their desk.

Worth asking any provider: *before you start, will you take a full copy of my site as it is
now, and will you give it back if I ask?*
