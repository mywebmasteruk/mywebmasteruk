Fourteen kinds of change, each marked with who decides it, how quickly it can be undone, and
what sets it off in the first place.

Six happen automatically. Six need a person to approve them. Two are never allowed at all:
anything touching prices, guarantees, legal wording or claims about the business, and anything
touching the settings that control whether Google shows the site.

That last group is the point of the whole table. Something that could change what a business
charges, or could remove it from Google with one wrong setting, should not exist — however well
it performs the rest of the time.
